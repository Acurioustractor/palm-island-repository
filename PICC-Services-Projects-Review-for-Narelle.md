## What this document is

This is a snapshot of every PICC service and project currently held in the Empathy Ledger. It is the foundation for the **2024–25 annual report build**, the **20-year celebration storytelling**, and the **always-on Empathy Ledger profile**.

It is not finished. It is **the start of a conversation**. The intention is for Narelle (and anyone else Rachel trusts) to walk through each entry, add reflections, correct anything that is wrong, fill the gaps, and tell us what stories or voices belong against each one.

For each service and project there are three places to add input:

- **Narelle's reflection** — anything you want to add, change, or push back on
- **Voices we should capture** — names of people whose stories we should record against this service or project
- **24/25 data we need** — specific numbers, dollars, milestones or stories needed for the annual report

At the end of the document there is a section called **What we still need to get done** — this is the practical list of things we need from PICC to finalise the 24/25 annual report.

# One-Page Summary

**For Rachel, Narelle, and anyone who wants the gist before the detail.**

## Where PICC sits in the Empathy Ledger right now

PICC is one of the first organisations in Australia to use the Empathy Ledger as its sovereign data and storytelling home. Today the ledger holds **28 services, 6 active projects, 839 quotable moments, 76 named storytellers, 123 transcripts and 452 themes** — all linked, all consent-governed, all queryable.

## The point of this document

We are two steps away from something powerful. This doc is the bridge.

**Step 1 — Confirm what's true.** Narelle walks through every service and project, corrects what's wrong, fills the gaps, names the voices, and flags the stories worth capturing.

**Step 2 — Chase the 24/25 data.** Once the list is right, we go after the 2024–25 financial and service-delivery numbers so the annual report and the 20-year celebration narrative are grounded in real, current evidence.

## What's strong

- Most services already have rich descriptions that read like the real story, not marketing copy
- Impact statistics captured against the flagship services (Bwgcolman Healing, Women's Shelter, Safe House, Community Justice Group, Diversionary Centre, Digital Service Centre, Retail)
- The Elders Trips and Storytelling project is already live on picc.studio with voices on Country
- Cultural protocols (consent, sensitivity, Elder approval workflows) are built into the foundation

## What's missing

- **5 services need descriptions**: Children and Family Centre, Family Care Service, Ferdies Haven, Palm Island Community Connection, Social Enterprises
- **~3 services may be missing entirely** — Narelle said about 31, we have 28
- **All the 24/25 headline numbers** — we only have 23/24 on file (income $23.4M, 197 staff, 2,283 medical clients, etc.)
- **Voice captures** for the annual report: Elder, staff lead, young person, Chair, CEO
- **Cultural sign-off** on Elder content before anything goes public
- **Photos and video** for most services

## The three most urgent things

1. **Aged Care Services** — flagged urgent in the ledger, Elders meeting 10 April 2026, council and mayor being pushed for action. Needs a clear line in the annual report and possibly a 20-year celebration ask.
2. **Blue Card Liaison Service** — funding cliff 30 June 2026, extension pending. Decision needed.
3. **The 24/25 financial figures** — nothing else can be finalised until these land.

## C — Youth, Education & Early Years

### 14. Bwgcolman Education Engagement Attainment Initiative (BEAI)

**Type:** Program · **Status:** Active

**Description:**
NIAA-funded education engagement program. Originally the Remote Student Attendance Strategy (RSAS) with Education Queensland — NIAA moved it to PICC. Roy put "Bwgcolman" in front of the name. Four service streams: in-school support, community engagement, communications, and rewards & recognition. Holistic approach from early childhood (CFC) through Bwgcolman Community School to YOS and PCYC. Focus: keeping the engaged engaged — working with the 60–100% attendance cohort. Engagement officers float between classrooms for individual student support. Fridays: boarding school enrolment support (working with MARUP) and community surveys. Spontaneous rewards system alongside end-of-term celebrations. Partners: St Michael's, Bwgcolman Community School, CFC, MARUP, PCYC. Coordinator: Tennille. Four engagement officers plus consultant (Tammy, PhD in engagement).
