# Narelle Interview Framework
## One conversation, four outputs

**Draft v1 · 9 April 2026**

**Feeds four outcomes from a single session:**

- 2024–25 Annual Report (audience-targeted)
- Always-on Empathy Ledger profile
- 20-Year Celebration and next-20 vision
- Bwgcolman Way case study (policy-grade)

**Companion documents:**

- PICC Services and Projects Review (for Narelle)
- PICC 20-Year Launchpad Plan

**For:** Ben, preparing the Narelle session · briefing Rachel afterwards
**Context:** Narelle is the single person who holds the operational memory of every PICC service and project. This framework is designed so that **one structured interview (or a short series)** produces everything we need for the 24/25 annual report, the always-on EL profile, the 20-year celebration narrative, and the Bwgcolman Way policy case study — without making her repeat herself.

It also doubles as **the spine of the workshop with Rachel**: the same questions that unlock Narelle's operational truth become the structure of the session that aligns Rachel on the next phase of the 20-year celebration and the Bwgcolman Way frame.

---

## How to use this document

**Before the interview:**
1. Send Narelle the services/projects review doc (`PICC-Services-Projects-Review-for-Narelle.md`) ahead of time — ask her to mark it up or just read it.
2. Print this framework. Record the conversation (with consent). Don't try to type live.
3. Every question is tagged with what it feeds: **[AR]** annual report · **[EL]** empathy ledger profile · **[20Y]** 20-year celebration · **[BW]** Bwgcolman Way case study.

**During the interview:**
- Follow Narelle's energy. If she goes deep on one service, let her.
- For each service/project she touches on, always ask the **three universal follow-ups** (see below).
- Mark unknowns clearly — don't guess, don't fill gaps on her behalf.

**After the interview:**
1. Feed answers back into the services review doc within 48 hours.
2. Fold the 20-year material into the Launchpad plan as Rachel alignment notes.
3. Pull the Bwgcolman Way answers into a separate case-study draft.

---

## The three universal follow-ups

Ask these for every service, project, or story Narelle raises. They unlock the most annual-report-ready material and take under a minute each:

1. **"Who should tell this story?"** — a named person whose voice should sit against this service in the Ledger.
2. **"What changed in 24/25?"** — new program, new partnership, new funding, new problem, new number.
3. **"If a funder read one thing about this service, what should it be?"** — forces the headline.

# Part A — Opening (10 minutes)

## A1. What's on your desk right now?
**[20Y, AR]**
> Before we go into any of the list — what's the thing that's taking up most of your head this week? What's the most urgent thing sitting with PICC right now?

*Why:* Anchors the conversation in the present. Often reveals a story that belongs front-and-centre in the annual report or the 20-year narrative.

## A2. The 10 April Elders meeting on Aged Care
**[AR, 20Y]**
> Aged Care Services is flagged urgent in the ledger. The Elders met on the 10th — how did it go? What's the ask on council and the mayor? Should Aged Care be a **feature** of the 20-year celebration as a forward commitment rather than a retrospective achievement?

*Why:* This is flagged as the single most urgent item in the services doc. The answer shapes whether Aged Care becomes a headline ask of the 20-year moment.

## A3. The big missing piece
**[AR, EL]**
> Narelle said there are about 31 services — we have 28 in the Ledger. What are the three we might be missing?

*Why:* Don't leave the interview without this. Everything else downstream assumes the service list is complete.

# Part D — Bwgcolman Way deep-dive (30 minutes)

**[BW, 20Y, AR]**

Bwgcolman Way (Delegated Authority for child safety) is the concrete proof point of the entire 20-year story. It deserves its own segment. The output of this segment is a 1,500-word policy-grade case study that becomes the anchor of the funder/sector briefing and the centrepiece of the annual report.

### The origin
- **D1.** When did the conversation about Delegated Authority first start? Who first said it was possible?
- **D2.** What existed before — what was the old system actually doing to Palm Island families?
- **D3.** Who fought for this, inside and outside PICC? Who said no, and how did that change?

### The mechanism
- **D4.** Walk me through a decision. A child safety case comes in — what happens now under Bwgcolman Way that didn't happen before?
- **D5.** What does "delegated authority sitting with PICC CEO rather than state director" actually mean in practice? What can Rachel sign off that she couldn't sign off before?
- **D6.** How does Bwgcolman Way connect to Safe House, Family Care Service, Family Participation Program, the CFC? What's the map?

### The evidence
- **D7.** How many cases have been decided locally since it started? What's the trajectory?
- **D8.** What are the outcome indicators that matter to PICC? (Not necessarily the state's indicators.)
- **D9.** Are there cases where the outcome would have been clearly different under the old system? (With anonymised consent.)

### The story
- **D10.** If you had to explain Bwgcolman Way to a federal minister in 60 seconds, what would you say?
- **D11.** If you had to explain it to a grandmother on Palm, what would you say?
- **D12.** Who should tell this story publicly? Who *shouldn't* — who needs to stay off the record?

### The next move
- **D13.** What's the next step for Bwgcolman Way? Health? Justice? Education? (The next-20 vision gives us: Delegated Authority expanded into health and justice by 2030.)
- **D14.** What would have to be true for that next move to work?

# Part E — 20-year celebration alignment (30 minutes)

**[20Y]**

This section is the live handshake between Narelle's operational truth and the 20-year Launchpad plan. The goal is to **test every assumption in the plan against what Narelle actually knows**, and to come out of the interview with a version of the next-20 vision that survives first contact with reality.

### The past 20 (2005 → 2025)
- **E1.** If you had to pick **three moments** from the last 20 years that define what PICC became, what would they be?
- **E2.** What's the thing PICC did that nobody outside Palm knows about — the win that didn't get written up?
- **E3.** Who's gone — Elders, staff, community members — whose voices need to be honoured in the celebration? (This is the voice capture priority list.)
- **E4.** Is there a year where PICC almost fell over? What held it together?

### The 20 voices for 20 years (E1 of the Launchpad plan)
We need **60 voices captured across 9 months**: one Elder, one staff, one young person per year 2005–2025.
- **E5.** Who's the **must-capture-now** list? Who can't wait?
- **E6.** Who needs to be asked by a specific person — who opens the door for us?
- **E7.** Which voices need full cultural sign-off before anything touches the public site?

### The next 20 (2025 → 2045)
- **E8.** If nothing changes, what does Palm look like in 20 years? What's PICC's job in stopping that?
- **E9.** Complete the sentence: **"In 20 years, every Palm Islander will have ___ because PICC built ___."**
- **E10.** What's the forward commitment that would make a funder or minister actually pay attention? (The Launchpad draft says: aged care on Palm by 2028, delegated authority into health and justice by 2030, sovereign story archive by 2045. Does that ring true? What's missing?)
- **E11.** If the 20-year celebration achieves one thing, what should it be?

### Cultural ownership
- **E12.** The plan has a principle: *"Ben and Nic behind the curtain — every public asset is in community voice."* Does that principle feel right? Is there a sharper version of it?
- **E13.** Who is the internal PICC second-in-command who can run this day-to-day? (Ben cannot be the single point of failure.)
- **E14.** Elder review cycle — 669 voices pending approval. What's the realistic weekly cadence? Who runs it?

### The ceremony and the rebrand
- **E15.** Where should the community ceremony happen? Who leads it?
- **E16.** Full rebrand or refresh? What does PICC lose if the brand changes too much? What does it lose if it doesn't change at all?
- **E17.** The book and the installation at The Centre — does a physical artefact feel right, or is there a different form community would respond to better?

# Part H — The Rachel workshop (after Narelle)

The Narelle interview becomes the material for the Rachel workshop. The same framework structures the session but the questions flip from **extraction** to **alignment**:

## Workshop spine (90 minutes with Rachel)

1. **The present (10 min)** — Walk Rachel through what Narelle said about the most urgent items (Aged Care, Blue Card, the three missing services).
2. **The service truth (15 min)** — Show Rachel the corrected services list. Get her sign-off on the groupings, the two PICC-only services (keep/retire/fold), and the three PICC-only projects (add to EL?).
3. **The 24/25 data plan (15 min)** — Walk through Part C. Confirm owners and deadlines. Name the weekly check-in cadence until the report ships.
4. **Bwgcolman Way as the anchor (20 min)** — Share the Bwgcolman Way deep-dive notes. Ask Rachel: is this the case study that carries the 20-year story? What would she add, change, reframe?
5. **The next-20 vision (20 min)** — Show her Narelle's answers to E8–E11. Ask Rachel to draft the vision statement live. **This is the single biggest gap in the current plan** — Rachel has to own the sentence.
6. **The Launchpad decisions (10 min)** — Green light on Approach A, date, rebrand vs refresh, internal 2IC, budget authorisation for the book + installation.

## What the workshop produces
- A next-20 vision statement in Rachel's words (first draft)
- An agreed 24/25 data capture plan with owners
- The Bwgcolman Way case study brief
- A confirmed voice capture priority list
- A date for the celebration
