# Part 1 — Key new facts NOT previously captured

## Legal framework (precise)

- Bwgcolman Way sits under **Part 2A of Queensland's Child Protection Act 1999**
- The Act allows the chief executive to delegate certain functions or powers for an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander child to a **prescribed delegate** only if that person is:
  - An Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander person
  - CEO of an appropriate Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander entity
  - Holds a working-with-children authority
  - Is considered suitable and appropriately qualified
- For Palm Island specifically: **the sole person delegated by the Director-General is PICC's CEO**
- The CEO makes decisions **collaboratively** with children and families and **in partnership** with Child Safety Services
- Delegations are made **child by child**, not as a blanket transfer
- Service design is **co-developed with the department**
- Children retain **legal review rights**

## Funding clarification (critical)

> **The $107.8M figure is likely the statewide delegated-authority investment envelope, not a Palm-only contract value.**

- PICC's live annual report places "$107.8 million over four years (2023-2028)" next to Bwgcolman Way
- A QFCC sector-insights paper uses the same $107.8M figure for delegated authority **more broadly across Queensland**
- Public Queensland expenditure datasets DO show PICC line items labelled "Delegated Authority - Palm Island" in 2023-24 and 2024-25
- **Recommendation:** Do NOT quote $107.8M as Palm-only funding in external communications until confirmed. Flag for the Narelle interview Part D.

## Statewide outcome numbers (context for Palm-specific asks)

- By 30 June 2024: Director-General had approved delegations for **197 children statewide** (functions: reunification, case planning, family contact, cultural connection)
- By 10 December 2025: **16 Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander entities** delivering 21 delegated-authority services across 26 CSSC&H, supporting **439 First Nations children statewide**
- Funding in place **until 30 June 2027**; evaluation planned by QATSICPP
- **Palm-specific numbers are NOT in the public record** — this is exactly what Narelle interview Part D7-D8 is designed to capture

These presentations show Bwgcolman Way is being positioned **not just as a local program, but as a broader example of community-controlled systems change**.

## The "Reclaiming our Storyline" blueprint context

- 10-year blueprint co-developed by QATSICPP and the Queensland department
- Explicitly ties delegated authority to: **healing, breaking cycles of trauma, honest truth-telling, reconnecting children and families to culture and community**
- This is the frame Bwgcolman Way inherits — not just administrative delegation, but a restorative/healing model

## Blue Card Liaison as enabling infrastructure

- July 2024: PICC launched a Blue Card Liaison pilot
- Helps people navigate screening for child-related work and kinship care
- ~20 positive notices per month
- Not Bwgcolman Way itself, but part of the enabling infrastructure for more localised child and family care

## The QATSICPP practice case study detail

- Describes PICC's system: Safe House, Family Participation, Family Wellbeing
- Local staff use **genealogy, cultural knowledge, and long-standing relationships** to rebuild trust with families who are fearful of government intervention
- Focus: keeping children on Country, reconnecting them with family, acting as bridge between families and department
- **"Bwgcolman Way does not look like a child-protection service dropped into Palm from outside. It looks more like a statutory extension of an existing Palm Island, community-controlled family-support ecosystem."**

## Community voice

- PICC's community-voices site includes: **"our cultural authority is recognised alongside the law"**
