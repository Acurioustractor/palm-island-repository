# Part 1 — The Vision

## What this report is

A small museum about a place.

Not an annual report in the usual sense. Not a stack of statistics. Not a glossy. A curated exhibition that walks the reader through Year 17 of Palm Island Community Company — six rooms, twelve museum objects per room type, one continuous experience.

The visitor enters through saltwater. Walks under a constellation. Sits with Elders. Sees the work. Leaves looking at the horizon.

## What this report does

- **Honours.** Names, faces, voices appear with consent. Cultural protocol is visible on the page.
- **Verifies.** Every number traces to Mark, the audit, the operations team, or a public source.
- **Holds.** What the year produced — Bwgcolman Way going live, the daycare rebuild, First 1,000 Days, the NDIS tripling, the Leadership Program designed.
- **Looks forward.** Three commitments for the next 20 years: Aged Care 2028. Bwgcolman Way Expanded 2030. Sovereign Story Archive 2045.

## What this report refuses

- Generic ATSICCO copy that could fit any organisation.
- Generated images of Aboriginal people. Ever.
- The old PICC palette (blue/purple/green/orange/teal). It's dead.
- "First in Australia." It is **first in Queensland.** $107.8M is **statewide,** not PICC-only.
- Pretending the AI wrote the words. Named editors did. The AI assisted.
- Hidden risks. Naming them is the precondition for navigating them.

## How this report reads

**Two registers.** Print is sparse, museum-grade, paper-led. Digital matches the print's dignity but layers in motion, audio, scroll-driven reveal. Same elements, same content, two render paths.

**Six rooms.**

1. Children & Families (Ochre)
2. Health & Wellbeing (Mangrove)
3. Justice & Safety (Coral)
4. Youth (Reef)
5. Economic (Star Gold)
6. Education & Community (Ocean)

Plus the framing: Cover, Acknowledgement, CEO + Chair messages, Board, Elders Group, Songline (Hull River), Year in Numbers, Atlas, Innovation, Leadership Program, Financials, Governance, Back cover.

**One walk.** Doorway → number → story → evidence → quiet, six times. Climax in the middle (CFC rebuild + reef cross-section). Longest exhale at the end (forward Atlas + back-cover constellation).

## The 6 atmospheric layers (run UNDER everything)

| # | Layer | Treatment |
|---|-------|-----------|
| 1 | **Sand-tone paper** | The page is never white. Sand `#FEF3C7` @ 18% opacity over warm cream. |
| 2 | **Constellation seed** | Star Gold @ 2.5% opacity, 9 dots, deterministic seed per page. Felt, not seen. |
| 3 | **Water grain** | Saltwater rings @ 3% opacity behind body copy zones — opt-in per element only. Globally disabled (too noisy). |
| 4 | **Corner-bracket rhythm** | Section colour @ 18% opacity, 0.5pt thickness, 12mm from trim. The invisible frame. |
| 5 | **Foot motif** | Wave line @ 18% section colour, page number inside small dot cluster. The tide-line. |
| 6 | **The Caveat hand** | One handwriting voice (Caveat font), one ink colour (Earth), one weight, throughout. The curator's pen. |

# Part 4 — The 12 Elements (reference cards)

For each element: anatomy, type stack, where it appears, variant rules.

## 1. Cartouche

**Purpose:** Section opener. The wall plaque at the entrance of each museum room.

**Anatomy:**
- Full A4 page in section colour @ 12% saturation
- Section name in Caveat 56pt
- Caveat sub-line beneath
- Thematic icon at 280pt in section colour @ 30% (or hero photo as photo-led variant)
- Corner brackets at 60% opacity
- Caveat sub-line + body promise lower-third
- Optional Roman numeral "Room i" top-centre

**Used at:** Pages 11, 13, 15, 16, 17, 18 (one per section).

**Variants:**
- Standard (icon-led): centred icon @ 30% behind type
- Photo-led: hero photo as upper band with section-colour wash @ 10% to integrate

## 2. Reliquary

**Purpose:** Single sacred number per anchor story. The artefact behind the glass.

**Anatomy:**
- Substrate wash (one of: stat-hero-horizon, saltwater rings, T3.1 Bwgcolman rings, T3.2 First-1000-Days arc, T3.4 BHS reef cross-section)
- Constellation seed @ 8% around the numeral
- Numeral in Caveat 130pt (compact: 96pt)
- Unit beneath in Inter caps, letter-spaced
- Star Marker beneath unit (the docent's pointer)
- Caveat hand annotation (12 words max)
- Optional caption in Inter caps muted

**Used at:** Pages 12, 14, plus Bookend close (4 total).

**Compact variant:** when two Reliquaries share a page. minHeight 220pt instead of 320pt.

## 3. Songline

**Purpose:** Horizontal narrative band crossing a full spread. Reads left-to-right like a scroll. RARE.

**Anatomy:**
- River-timeline OR three-bands wash as substrate
- Wave line threading through at mid-height
- 3-7 waypoints, alternating above/below
- Each waypoint: small dot + vertical hairline + date (Inter caps) + place name (Caveat) + optional photo + note

**Used at:** Pages 7-8 (Hull River) + optionally 14-15 (CFC rebuild).

**Songlines stay rare or they stop singing.** Two maximum in the report.

**Variants:**
- `hullRiver` — Ocean → Reef → Mangrove palette (return-to-Country)
- `cfcRebuild` — Earth → Coral → Sand palette (recovery)

## 4. Lantern

**Purpose:** Elder voice. The hush of the exhibition. Always full-bleed left or right edge — Elders speak from the side of the room, not the lectern.

**Anatomy:**
- Sand `#FEF3C7` panel, full-bleed off chosen edge
- Elder-quote-wash substrate @ 18%
- Small turtle-red 9-dot cluster top-left as anchor
- Opening quote mark in Caveat 56pt at 40% turtle red
- The quote in Caveat 22pt italic-style, max 420pt wide
- Attribution in Caveat 16pt turtle red
- Role in Inter 8pt
- Optional 32pt circular portrait inset
- Cultural-review consent footer in Inter 6.5pt muted

**Used at:** Page 21 (4 lanterns, alternating left/right).

**Invariant by design:** Lanterns refuse section colour. The wash stays warm-ochre regardless of which section's room they sit in. Cultural rule made visible. Do not parameterise away.

## 5. Hearth

**Purpose:** Where community voices gather. Voice meets evidence.

**Anatomy:**
- Shell-warmer panel `#FBF6E4`
- Section-colour top border (2pt)
- Portrait left (72pt × 72pt, 2pt rounded), or PersonAvatar fallback (initials in section colour)
- Quote in Caveat 13pt italic-style (right of portrait)
- Star Marker beside name (section colour)
- Name in Inter 10pt bold
- Role in Inter 8pt driftwood, with optional date suffix
- Consent footer in Inter 6.5pt muted

**Used at:** Pages 22 (six hearths in 2-column 3-row grid), plus Bookend close.

**Youth variant:** double Star Marker beside name (constellation thickens for young voices).

## 6. Horizon

**Purpose:** Forward-looking vision. Eye pulls upward.

**Anatomy:**
- stat-hero-horizon wash at panel base (lower 60%; upper 40% clear)
- Top row: title left, year stamp right
- Section-colour gradient bar dividing speaker from year
- Vision statement in Caveat 16pt italic-style
- Detail line in Inter 9pt driftwood
- Optional attribution in Caveat 12pt section colour

**Used at:** Page 23 (3 horizons stacked — 2028, 2030, 2045 forward commitments).

## 7. Atlas

**Purpose:** Services-around-island map. The geography of what PICC does.

**Anatomy:**
- services-around-island substrate (or T3.5 generated equivalent)
- Constellation overlay @ 10%, 20 dots
- 24 service nodes plotted as small concentric-style markers in section colours
- Anchor services magnitude 3 (larger), regular services magnitude 2
- Hairline connectors between kindred services
- Inter 7pt labels (bold for anchors)
- Optional Caveat hand note for services

**Used at:** Pages 19-20 (Orientation Atlas — descriptive of today), Page 23-close (Closing Atlas — same coordinates with 2028 commitments mapped).

## 8. Specimen

**Purpose:** Scientific-feeling comparative panel. Before/after.

**Anatomy:**
- before-after-hull (infrastructure) OR reef-layers (cultural/health) wash substrate
- Two states side-by-side, each as a column
- Thin vertical gradient bar dividing them
- Each column: optional photo at top, label in Inter caps, value in Caveat 26pt, detail in Inter 9pt
- Caveat annotation beneath with hairline-arrow pointer

**Used at:** Pages 9, 15, 17 (before/after at three points: CFC water receding, BHS reef cross-section, Blue Card key).

## 9. Kuling Field

**Purpose:** Constellation of small stats. The night sky as data.

**Anatomy:**
- Sand-pulled-toward-earth panel `#F4EFE0`
- Dense ConstellationPattern overlay @ 18%, 32 dots
- Title block top-left (Inter caps + Caveat sub-line)
- Stars positioned absolutely in 200pt-tall field
- Each star: StarMarker (size = magnitude 1-5) + Caveat numeral + Inter caps label
- Hairline connectors forming informal constellations

**Used at:** Pages 12 (NDIS), 18 (youth), 20 (economic).

**Magnitude maps to brightness.** The biggest number is the brightest star.

## 10. Vitrine + Triptych

**Purpose:** Display case for a single fact. The page paper is the glass; the brackets are the frame.

**Anatomy:**
- 31% width (in triptych) or 48% width (in pair)
- Corner brackets at 60% section colour (or 9-dot cluster top-left for elder variant)
- Caveat 36pt value (centred)
- Inter 9pt label (centred caps)
- Optional Caveat 11pt caption
- Hairline rule beneath at 40% width centred

**Used at:** Section openers (3 vitrines after each Cartouche), Year in Numbers, Innovation, Financials, Leadership Program.

**Variants:**
- `standard` — corner brackets at 60%
- `elder` — single 9-dot cluster upper-left only (Turtle Red)
- `community` — corner brackets + wave line beneath

## 11. Fold + Pair

**Purpose:** A consented, named photograph treated as a museum plate.

**Anatomy:**
- Photo with overflow:hidden, 8pt rounded corners
- White corner brackets @ 85% inset 8pt over the photo
- Caveat 14pt caption ranged left
- Inter 9pt bold caps name beneath caption
- Inter 6.5pt consent metadata right-aligned
- Section-colour gradient bar at base (width by shape)

**Used at:** 18 photo slots throughout the report.

**Three sub-shapes:**
- `portrait` — closer crop, name larger (PersonAvatar register)
- `landscape` — wider gradient bar (place/event register)
- `country` — no person attribution, place-name in Caveat (Country shots)

## 12. Margin Note

**Purpose:** The curator's whisper. The hand-written note in the margin.

**Anatomy:**
- Three diminishing dots forming a "see-also" mark
- Single line of Caveat 13pt in Earth or Ochre
- Optional thin connector hairline
- Max 12 words

**Used at:** 1-2 per spread, never on Cartouche or Lantern pages (those are too sacred for whispers).

**The most consistent voice in the report.** One hand, one tone, throughout.

## §5.3 Story Library (short narrative paragraphs, ready to plug)

Pre-written paragraphs ready for any element that needs them.

### S-001 · Cover narrative

*A small museum about a place. Not an annual report in the usual sense. A curated exhibition that walks the reader through Year 17 of Palm Island Community Company.*

### S-002 · Acknowledgement of Country

*We acknowledge the Manbarra people as the Traditional Custodians of Palm Island, and the Bwgcolman people — the descendants of the more than forty First Nations forcibly relocated here from across Queensland between 1914 and 1972. We pay our respects to Elders past, present, and emerging. We honour the strength of those who came before us, the wisdom of those who walk with us today, and the future we are building with our children.*

*Bwgcolman — many tribes, one people.*

### S-003 · Bwgcolman Way anchor

*In 2024-25, the Child Protection Act 1999 was used in a way it has never been used on Palm Island before. Under Part 2A of the Act — prescribed delegates for Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander children — the Queensland Government delegated statutory child-protection functions to the Chief Executive of an Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Community Controlled Organisation. PICC became the first ATSICCO in Queensland to receive that authority.*

*Decisions about whether a Palm Island child is removed, where they are placed, and how their family stays connected to them are no longer made by the state director of child safety in Brisbane. They are made on Palm Island. By Palm Islanders. Guided by our blueprint, Reclaiming our Storyline (April 2023), which the Queensland Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Child Protection Peak co-developed with the Department of Child Safety, Seniors and Disability Services.*

### S-004 · CFC daycare rebuild

*The Children and Family Centre arrived on Palm Island by barge in June 2012. The CFC is the front door of PICC's family services — early childhood, primary health, family support, all under one roof. In late 2023 and early 2024, severe weather and flooding damaged the building and destroyed equipment. The rebuild is the story of FY24-25.*

*The team came back. Not "rebuilt" as in restored. Rebuilt as in came back stronger. Hailey Jane Wetzel and her team are the reason. Local employment is what made the rebuild possible. Outside contractors would not have been on hand the day the water dropped. The CFC is more than a building. The team is the building.*

### S-005 · Bwgcolman Healing Service rename

*In January 2024, after extensive consultation with the Elders' Advisory Group, the Primary Health Centre was renamed the Bwgcolman Healing Service. Bwgcolman — many tribes, one people. Healing — what we do, in the way our people understand the word. The renaming was symbolic. The work behind it is not.*

### S-006 · First 1,000 Days launch

*In April 2024, just before this fiscal year began, PICC opened the doors on the First 1,000 Days Program. A child health nurse, an Aboriginal health worker, and a GP, working alongside the Bwgcolman Healing Service, walking with families through the most formative period of a child's life — from conception to age two.*

*The framing comes from Professor Kerry Arabena's 2014 Medical Journal of Australia paper, the founding text of First 1000 Days Australia. We adopted the model and built the Palm Island version: a small, integrated team, deeply linked to early childhood (CFC) and to primary health (Bwgcolman Healing).*

### S-007 · Hull River journey (epilogue framing)

*In late 2025, after the close of the fiscal year this report covers, a group of Elders travelled from Palm Island back to the Hull River, near Mission Beach. We include the journey here because it is the natural epilogue to FY24-25 — the work the year was building toward. For some, it was a return to family stories told but never seen. For others, it was the first time setting foot on land their ancestors had been forcibly removed from a hundred years before.*

### S-008 · PIC Leadership Program

*The PIC Leadership Program is PICC's own, in-house, accredited leadership program — designed by PICC staff, for PICC staff, building on the lived experience of running 24 services in a remote community. It does what no off-the-shelf program does: it teaches leadership in PICC's specific terms.*

*Initiated in 2024-25 by PICC's existing leadership group. Co-designed with staff across services. Funded by DSS. First cohort begins May 2026. It is the most important investment Year 17 made.*

### S-009 · Risks acknowledgement

*Naming a risk is not admitting failure. It is the precondition for navigating one. Publishing them publicly is an act of accountability to community, members, and partners.*

### S-010 · Innovation panel

*15 years of PICC reporting, digitised, indexed, and searchable in seconds. Three local photographers. Hundreds of images. A community-controlled digital archive making the Empathy Ledger work for everyone. Made on Palm Island. Owned on Palm Island.*

### S-011 · Closing reflection

*Three commitments. Three generations. Aged Care on Palm Island so our Elders never have to leave Country. Bwgcolman Way expanded into health and justice. Every Palm Island story captured, consented, and held by us.*

*Our children are our future. They are our ancestors of tomorrow. Each of them have a role in growing our community and keeping our culture strong. We've gotta make it right for them.*
